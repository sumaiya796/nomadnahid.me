Performance Max for e-commerce: what to set up before you scale

By Md Nahid Hossain · 28 November 2026 · 6 min read

Performance Max can be a strong campaign type for online stores. It can also spend a lot of money quickly on the wrong things if the foundations are weak. Before you raise the budget, these are the pieces I check, in roughly this order.

What Performance Max actually does

Performance Max (PMax) is a single campaign that can show your products and ads across Google's channels: Shopping results, Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps. You give it a goal, a budget, your product feed, creative assets and some hints about your audience. Google's automation decides where and when to show your ads.

That automation is only as good as what you feed it. In practice, three inputs make most of the difference: your conversion data, your product feed and the structure you give the campaign.

1. Make sure purchase tracking is accurate

PMax optimises toward the conversions you mark as primary. For an online store, that should be completed purchases with the real order value. If add-to-cart or page views are also set as primary, the campaign may chase cheap actions instead of sales.

Check that:

  • Purchases are tracked once per order, with order ID, value and currency.
  • Only the purchase action is primary for the campaign's goal; supporting actions are secondary.
  • Enhanced Conversions is switched on, so more sales can be matched back to ads.
  • The conversion numbers in Google Ads are close to the orders in your store for the same period.

If those numbers are far apart, fix tracking first. Scaling a campaign that learns from wrong data only makes the problem bigger.

2. Clean up your Merchant Center feed

For e-commerce, the product feed in Google Merchant Center is the heart of PMax. Your product titles and images are often the ad itself, especially in Shopping results.

  • Titles: lead with what the shopper searches for: product type, brand, key attributes such as size, colour or material.
  • Images: clear, well-lit product images that follow Google's image rules. Disapproved images mean disapproved products.
  • Identifiers: correct GTINs and brand where they exist.
  • Product type and categories: fill them in properly; they help both Google and your own reporting.
  • Shipping and returns: set them up accurately in Merchant Center. Missing or wrong shipping information can cause products to be limited or disapproved.

Then look at the diagnostics in Merchant Center. Every disapproved or limited product is one less product that can sell. Fixing these issues is often the cheapest growth available.

3. Use custom labels to group products by business value

Not every product deserves the same budget. Custom labels in your feed let you tag products by margin, price range, best sellers, seasonal items or clearance stock. You can then use those labels to split products into separate asset groups or campaigns.

A simple, useful starting point is to separate your best sellers or highest-margin products from the rest. That way, the products that make you the most money are not competing for budget with items that rarely sell.

4. Structure asset groups around themes

Each asset group combines a set of products (a listing group) with creative assets: headlines, descriptions, images, logos and, ideally, video. Group products that share a theme and an audience, such as one product line or category, so the headlines and images can speak directly to that shopper.

Avoid putting your whole catalogue into one asset group with generic text. Equally, do not create dozens of tiny asset groups that never get enough data to learn. A handful of meaningful groups is usually easier to manage and read.

5. Give the campaign good audience signals

Audience signals are hints, not strict targeting. They help PMax find the right people faster, especially in the early weeks. The strongest signals are your own data:

  • Customer lists (uploaded with consent).
  • Website visitors and past purchasers.
  • Custom segments built from the search terms your buyers use.

Add interest and demographic signals if they fit your customers, but your first-party data is the most valuable hint you can give.

6. Protect your brand traffic and control waste

PMax can capture searches for your own brand name, which may make results look better than they really are. People searching for your brand were likely to buy anyway. Use brand exclusions if you want PMax to focus on new demand, and run a separate brand Search campaign if brand traffic matters to you.

Review search term insights and placement reports regularly. Where your account offers negative keyword controls for PMax, use them to block clearly irrelevant searches, such as job seekers or searches for products you do not sell.

7. Choose a sensible bidding strategy

For stores, PMax usually bids on conversion value. A practical approach is:

  1. Start with Maximize conversion value without a target, so the campaign can gather data.
  2. Once it has a steady history of purchases, consider adding a target ROAS based on what the campaign is actually achieving and what your margins allow.
  3. Adjust targets and budgets in moderate steps. Large, sudden changes can push the campaign back into a learning phase and make performance unstable.

If you are mainly trying to win new customers rather than repeat buyers, look at the new customer acquisition goal, which lets you bid higher for new customers or focus on them only.

8. Check the landing pages and the store itself

Ads can only send people to your store. Whether they buy depends on the product page: price, shipping costs, delivery times, reviews, page speed and how easy checkout is on a phone. If many people click but few buy, it is often a website issue, not a campaign issue.

Also review the Final URL expansion setting. It lets Google send traffic to other pages on your site. That can help, but you may want to exclude pages such as blog posts, policies or careers pages that will not convert.

9. Know how you will judge results

Before scaling, agree on the numbers that matter: return on ad spend, cost per purchase, or profit after ad costs. Look at results over enough time to smooth out daily swings, and compare against your store's own sales data, not just what Google Ads reports.

The channel performance and asset reports in PMax help you see where the campaign is spending and which assets are working. Use them to refresh weak images and headlines rather than rebuilding the campaign every week.

A simple pre-scale checklist

  • Purchases tracked once, with value, and matching store orders closely.
  • Only purchases set as the primary conversion.
  • Merchant Center feed clean, with key products approved.
  • Products grouped by theme or business value using custom labels.
  • Asset groups with strong images, headlines and, ideally, video.
  • Audience signals built from your own customer and website data.
  • Brand traffic handled deliberately.
  • Budget and ROAS targets changed in moderate steps.

When all of these are in place, scaling becomes a calmer, more predictable process. That is how I approach PMax for online stores: tracking and feed first, then structure, then budget.
