Google Ads for local service businesses: getting more calls and booked jobs

By Md Nahid Hossain · 28 November 2026 · 6 min read

For a plumber, HVAC company, cleaner, dentist or repair shop, a click is worth nothing on its own. What matters is the phone ringing and the job being booked. Here is how I set up Google Ads for local service businesses so the budget goes toward real calls and booked jobs.

Start with what a good lead looks like

Before touching the account, write down what a valuable lead is for your business. For most local services it is one of these:

  • A phone call long enough to be a real conversation.
  • A completed quote or booking form with real contact details.
  • A booked appointment in your scheduling tool.
  • For some businesses, a WhatsApp or chat message.

Also note what is not a lead: job seekers, suppliers trying to sell to you, people outside your service area, and people looking for do-it-yourself help. Knowing both lists makes every later decision easier.

Track calls properly, not just clicks

Calls are often the most valuable action for a local business, and they are also the easiest to track badly. There are three types worth setting up:

  1. Calls from ads: add call assets to your campaigns and use Google's call reporting, so calls made directly from the ad are recorded with their duration.
  2. Calls from your website: people who click an ad, visit your site and then call. Google Ads can track these with a website call conversion, which swaps your number for a Google forwarding number for ad visitors.
  3. Clicks on your phone number: tracked in Google Tag Manager as a supporting signal. A click is not the same as a completed call, so I usually keep this as a secondary action.

Set a sensible minimum call length for counting a call as a conversion. Google's default is 60 seconds. Adjust it to fit your business, so a short wrong-number call is not counted as a lead.

Track forms and bookings once

For forms, track the successful submission, not the button click, and count one conversion per person. For booking tools, track the confirmed appointment wherever possible. If your booking system runs on a different domain, it may need its own tracking setup so bookings are not lost between the two sites.

Test every action in Google Tag Manager's Preview mode before relying on it. I would rather spend an hour testing than spend a month optimising toward broken data.

Build campaigns around services, not one big ad group

One of the most common problems in local accounts is a single ad group covering every service. Emergency repairs, routine maintenance and new installations attract different searches, different urgency and different value. When they share one ad group, the ads cannot match any of them well.

Instead, create campaigns or ad groups per service, each with:

  • Keywords specific to that service.
  • Ads that mention that service in the headline.
  • A landing page about that service, not just the home page.

This makes the ads more relevant, often improves click-through rate, and lets you put more budget behind the services that bring the best jobs.

Choose high-intent keywords and block the rest

Local service searches with strong intent usually include the service plus words like "repair", "near me", "emergency", "install", "quote" or your city name. Broad informational searches, such as "how does a boiler work", rarely turn into booked jobs.

Build a solid negative keyword list from day one. Common candidates include "jobs", "careers", "salary", "training", "course", "DIY", "how to", "free" and "parts", plus competitor names if you do not want to bid on them. Then review the search terms report regularly and keep adding negatives. This habit alone often saves a noticeable share of wasted spend.

Get location targeting right

Target only the areas you actually serve. In the location settings, choose to show ads to people in or regularly in your targeted locations, rather than people who have only shown interest in them. Otherwise, you may pay for clicks from people far outside your service area.

If some zip codes or towns bring better jobs than others, use location bid adjustments or separate campaigns to put more budget there. Location performance reports show where your leads come from.

Match your ad schedule to when you can answer

A call that nobody answers is a wasted click and often a lost customer. Run call-focused ads only when someone is available to pick up, or make sure missed calls are returned quickly. If you offer emergency service at night, make that clear in your ads and keep those hours covered.

Use assets and your Google Business Profile

Ad assets make your ads larger and more useful. For local businesses, the most helpful are:

  • Call assets for one-tap calling.
  • Location assets, linked from your Google Business Profile, so people see your address and distance.
  • Sitelinks to key services, reviews or a booking page.
  • Callouts for things like licensed and insured, same-day service or free estimates, only if they are true.

Keep your Google Business Profile complete and up to date, with correct hours, services and photos. It supports both your ads and your organic local presence. In some countries and categories, Google's Local Services Ads are also worth testing alongside Search campaigns.

Make the landing page easy for a phone user

Most local service searches happen on mobile. The landing page should load quickly, show your phone number at the top as a tap-to-call button, explain the service in plain words, show the areas you cover, and include trust signals such as reviews, licences and guarantees you actually offer. A short form for people who prefer not to call helps too.

Connect leads to booked jobs

A form fill or a call is a lead, not a job. If your CRM or job management tool records which leads became paying customers, you can send that information back to Google Ads through offline conversion imports or Enhanced Conversions for leads. Then the bidding system can learn from booked jobs, not just enquiries.

Even without a full integration, a simple monthly check helps: how many leads did ads bring, how many were real, and how many became jobs? That tells you whether to adjust keywords, services or budgets.

Choose bidding once tracking is solid

New accounts or campaigns without conversion history often start with Maximize Clicks or Maximize Conversions while data builds up. Once you have a steady flow of tracked leads, Target CPA can help control cost per lead. Change targets gradually and give the campaign time to adjust.

Quick checklist

  • Calls from ads and from your website tracked, with a sensible minimum duration.
  • Forms and bookings tracked once, and tested.
  • Campaigns or ad groups organised by service, with matching landing pages.
  • High-intent keywords and a growing negative keyword list.
  • Location set to people in or regularly in your service area.
  • Ads running when someone can answer the phone.
  • Call, location and sitelink assets in place.
  • Booked jobs fed back into Google Ads where possible.

This is the system I build for service businesses: clean tracking first, then campaigns shaped around the services and areas that bring real jobs.
